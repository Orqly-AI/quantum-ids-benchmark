The narrower positive the audit left, the four-qubit hybrid's higher detection than the tuned Random
Forest at the $1\%$-$\fpr$ operating point on NSL-KDD ($p=0.005$, BH $q=0.030$), is classical as well
(Section~\ref{sec:kattr}). It was strongest for the smallest circuit and vanished at twelve qubits, which
we had read as implicit regularisation rather than expressivity; the classical twins confirm that reading
and remove the quantum layer from it. About half of the margin is the input, since a Random Forest on the
hybrid's four features closes it by half, and nearly all of the rest is the neural model class: a tanh
layer of the same width, or no middle layer at all, in the same pipeline reproduces it, and the gain lies
in novel attack types, to which small neural networks on four principal components extrapolate better
than tree ensembles. It reproduces on three IBM Heron processors, so it is not an artefact of noiseless
simulation, but neither is it quantum. For variational models the lesson is that the control attribution
requires is a classical twin that replaces only the quantum layer; a strong classical baseline on
different inputs, however well tuned, cannot supply it.
