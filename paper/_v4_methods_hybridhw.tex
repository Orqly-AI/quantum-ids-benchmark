The same devices also run the variational model behind the audit's other surviving positive, the
four-qubit hybrid of Table~\ref{tab:audit}. Its trained circuit (angle encoding, three strongly
entangling layers) is rebuilt in Qiskit with the trained weights bound as constants and the four input
angles as circuit parameters (statevector expectations agree with PennyLane to $10^{-15}$), transpiled to
the heavy-hex coupling map (depth 84 to 92, 30 two-qubit gates), and its four $\langle Z_i\rangle$ are
measured at 128 shots on three disjoint, seeded, class-balanced subsets of 2{,}000 test rows, one job per
subset and device, so that every device sees the same 6{,}000 rows, 3{,}000 of them benign. The measured
expectations pass through the trained classical head unchanged, so only the quantum layer is replaced.
The model is retrained from the released configuration and seed before the runs; the retrained model
reproduces the published test scores to $2\times10^{-8}$. We compare it on the same rows with the exact
simulation, the tuned Random Forest of Table~\ref{tab:audit} and the classical twins described next, by
paired bootstrap.

\paragraph{Classical twins of the hybrid.} To attribute the hybrid's result we keep its pipeline intact
(the four PCA features, the seeded 2{,}000-row training subsample, the $\mathrm{Linear}(4,4)$ and $\tanh$
input layer, the $\mathrm{Linear}(4,2)$ output layer, Adam at learning rate $0.005$, 20 epochs, batches
of 64, class-weighted cross-entropy, and seeds 42 to 46) and replace only the quantum layer. With $R_Y$
angle encoding, every $\langle Z_j\rangle$ of the circuit is a trigonometric polynomial in the four input
angles $a_i$ with frequencies in $\{-1,0,1\}$~\cite{schuld2021encoding}, that is, a linear combination of
the $3^4=81$ products $\prod_i f_i(a_i)$ with $f_i\in\{1,\cos a_i,\sin a_i\}$. The \emph{Fourier twin}
replaces the circuit by an unconstrained linear map on these 81 features, a strict superset of the
circuit's function class; the \emph{MLP twin} by two $\tanh$ layers of width four (40 parameters against
the circuit's 36); and a third twin removes the middle layer altogether. Fitting the 81 coefficients to a
trained circuit by least squares tests the expansion directly. To separate the model class from the
input, we also train the Random Forest on the hybrid's own input: its four features, on the full
training file and on the same 2{,}000 rows.
