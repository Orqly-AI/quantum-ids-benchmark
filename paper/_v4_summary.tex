\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:ksum}
No quantum advantage survives attribution in this benchmark. The audit's two positives, each significant
after correction, were produced classically: the quantum kernel's by its surrogate's confounds and then by
a bandwidth grid too narrow for a distribution shift that cross-validation cannot see, and the hybrid's by
the input and the small neural pipeline around its quantum layer, which a tanh layer of the same width
reproduces. Three findings of general use come out of the exercise. First, the selection failure is
caused by train-to-test shift: it is absent without shift, recreated on three datasets by holding attack
families out of training, including a spurious $0.17$ quantum lead, and tracked, coarsely, by a
label-free MMD; no training-only rule repairs it in general, so comparisons under shift should be
reported under several selection rules with the oracle bound. Second, both quantum models are classically
reproducible for identifiable reasons: the IQP kernel from its own circuit phases on every dataset, by the
geometric difference, and the hybrid's trained circuit as an exact 81-term trigonometric polynomial.
Third, both run on current hardware without changing these conclusions: on three IBM Heron processors the
projected kernel stays within $0.01$ ROC-AUC of simulation, and the hybrid's low-false-positive detection
is unchanged on 6{,}000 rows per device, as is its tie with its classical twin.
