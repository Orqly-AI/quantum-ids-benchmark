The device runs also test the hybrid. Its four expectations, measured at 128 shots on the same 6{,}000
class-balanced test rows on each device (three disjoint subsets of 2{,}000, one job each), correlate with
their exact values at $r=0.972$ to $0.977$ (Table~\ref{tab:hybridhw}). ROC-AUC falls from $0.893$ to
between $0.869$ and $0.876$ ($p<0.001$ on every device), of which shot noise alone accounts for $0.012$ in
simulation of the first subset, while detection at $1\%$ false positives is unchanged: $0.505$ to $0.517$
against $0.513$ exact ($p\ge0.76$). The margin over the Random Forest on these rows, $+0.132$ to $+0.144$
($p=0.003$ to $0.012$), therefore survives real device noise on three processors. It is also the margin
the classical twins show on the same rows ($+0.136$ to $+0.153$), and the hardware hybrid does not differ
from the tanh twin on any device ($-0.009$ to $+0.004$, $p\ge0.47$), so the result of
Section~\ref{sec:kattr} holds on hardware as well. Pooling was necessary: on a single 2{,}000-row subset
the $1\%$ threshold rests on ten benign scores, and the forest's detection varied from $0.304$ to $0.517$
across the three subsets, its deficit to the hybrid from $0.21$ to zero. The nine jobs used 86 to 87
QPU-seconds each, about $0.04$ QPU-seconds per sample.
