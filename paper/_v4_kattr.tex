\subsection{The variational positive is classical}\label{sec:kattr}
The audit's second surviving positive is the four-qubit hybrid's higher detection than the tuned Random
Forest at $1\%$ false positives on NSL-KDD (Section~\ref{sec:op}). The Random Forest was the strongest
classical model on that metric, but it is not a control for the hybrid: it is a different model class,
it sees eight features rather than four, and it trains on the whole training file rather than on the
hybrid's 2{,}000 rows. Table~\ref{tab:attrib} holds all of that fixed except the quantum layer
(Section~\ref{sec:kdiag}), and adds the Random Forest on the hybrid's own input.

The trained circuit is classical in a precise sense. Fitting the 81-term trigonometric expansion to the
circuit trained with seed 42 reproduces its four expectations to $2\times10^{-15}$ on held-out angles, and
the classical model built from those coefficients reproduces the hybrid's test scores to $4\times10^{-7}$,
the precision of single-precision arithmetic, with identical metrics. Entanglement is not idle, since
without the 16 four-way products the error rises to $0.044$, but every term it contributes can be written
down. Trained from scratch in the same pipeline, the classical twins also match what the hybrid learns
where it matters. Over five seeds the hybrid detects $0.515\pm0.007$ of attacks at $1\%$ false positives,
the tanh twin $0.511\pm0.006$, the Fourier twin $0.506\pm0.016$ and the network with no middle layer at
all $0.506\pm0.010$, against $0.467$ for the published Random Forest. The hybrid never differs
significantly from the tanh twin (0 of 5 seeds) and leads the other two twins on 2 of 5 seeds each, by at
most $0.021$. On ROC-AUC the network without a middle layer is ahead of the hybrid ($0.914$ against
$0.899$).

The margin over the published forest therefore has classical parts, and the Random Forest controls size
them. About half is the input: the same forest on the hybrid's four features detects $0.491$, and the
hybrid's lead over it shrinks to $0.023$ to $0.030$, significant on one of three seeds. Most of the rest is
the model class, which a tanh layer supplies ($0.511$), and the quantum layer adds $0.004$ over that layer,
within noise. The split by attack type shows where the neural models differ. On attacks of types present
in training every model detects $0.60$ to $0.65$ at $1\%$ false positives; on the 3{,}750 attacks of the
novel types the neural models detect $0.24$ to $0.25$ and the forest at most $0.15$, whatever input it is
given. The one variational result that survived the audit is the behaviour of a small, class-weighted
neural network on four principal components, which extrapolates to novel attacks at a strict alarm
budget better than a tree ensemble, and the quantum layer adds nothing to it that a tanh layer of the
same width does not.
